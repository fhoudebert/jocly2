Seirawan++

Seirawan++ is an extension of Seirawan Chess that allows players to become familiar with a wide variety of fairy pieces on a standard chessboard.

Seirawan++ Chess is played on a standard 8 × 8 chessboard and follows the normal rules of chess, with two additional pieces selected during the opening phase.

Introducing the Pieces

When a player moves for the first time a piece that is still on its original square, they may, during the same move, place one of their reserve pieces on the square that has just been vacated.

Placing a reserve piece is optional. Each starting piece (King, Queen, Rooks, Bishops and Knights) can vacate its original square only once: therefore, a player has eight opportunities to introduce their two reserve pieces. If they move all their pieces from the first rank without placing a reserve piece, any remaining reserve pieces can no longer be introduced.

A reserve piece cannot be introduced as a way of getting out of check.

Castling

When castling, a player may place one of their reserve pieces on the original square vacated by the King or the Rook. However, only one of the two reserve pieces may be introduced during that move.

Promotion

When a pawn reaches the last rank, it may be promoted to a piece of the player's choice.

Additional Pieces

Marshall

Combines the movement of a Rook and a Knight.

Cardinal

Combines the movement of a Bishop and a Knight.

Kirin

Moves one square diagonally like a Ferz, or jumps two squares orthogonally like a Dabbaba. Betza notation: FD.

Phoenix

Moves one square orthogonally like a Wazir, or jumps two squares diagonally like an Alfil. Betza notation: WA.

Badger

Slides diagonally like a Bishop, or jumps like a Dabbaba. Betza notation: BD, hence its name BaDger.

Ram

Slides orthogonally like a Rook, or jumps like an Alfil. Betza notation: RA, hence its name RAm.

Griffon

This piece can appear through the promotion of a Ship. It moves one square diagonally and can then slide any number of squares vertically or horizontally.

Rhinoceros

This piece can appear through the promotion of a Snake. It moves one square orthogonally and can then slide any number of squares diagonally.

Elephant

Moves either one or two squares diagonally, forwards or backwards. It can jump over the first square if it is occupied, in order to reach the second diagonal square.

Cannon

Moves like a Rook. To capture an enemy piece, the Cannon must be aligned with it along a rank or file, never diagonally, with exactly one other piece, friendly or enemy, between them. The intervening piece "charges" the Cannon, which can then capture the enemy piece. In this case, the Cannon takes the enemy piece's square by jumping over the intervening piece, which remains in place. The Cannon is borrowed from Xiangqi.

Centaur

Combines the movement of a King and a Knight.

Snake

Moves one square vertically and can then slide diagonally.

Ship

Moves one square diagonally and can then slide vertically, but not horizontally.

Hawk

Jumps diagonally or orthogonally by 2 or 3 squares.

Mammoth

Jumps orthogonally (horizontally or vertically) and diagonally by 1 or 2 squares.

Machine

The orthogonal equivalent of the Elephant; it can jump over the first square.
