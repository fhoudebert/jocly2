Two players, Cho (green, moves first) and Han (red).

To win, you must checkmate your opponent's General.

Board

A 9 × 10 grid. Pieces stand on the intersections, not in the squares. Each side has a 3 × 3 palace marked by its diagonals. Unlike Chinese chess, there is no river: every file runs unbroken from one end of the board to the other.

Pieces

For each side: 1 General, 2 Guards, 2 Elephants, 2 Horses, 2 Chariots, 2 Cannons, 5 Soldiers. The General starts in the centre of the palace.

The palace diagonals

Every piece except the Horse and the Elephant may travel along the diagonal lines drawn in a palace. Those lines only join the corners to the centre, so the middle of each palace edge has no diagonal.

Moves

  • General and Guard move one step along any line of the palace, and may never leave it. Both move alike; only the General can be checked.
  • Chariot moves like a rook. Inside a palace it may also slide one or two steps along a diagonal, from a corner to the centre, from the centre to a corner, or from one corner to the opposite one.
  • Cannon moves like a chariot but must jump exactly one piece, the screen, on every move - capture or not. It may neither jump over nor capture another Cannon. On a palace diagonal it can only go from one corner to the opposite one, over whatever stands on the centre. At the start of the game no Cannon can move at all.
  • Horse moves one step orthogonally then one step diagonally outwards, and is blocked if the first step is occupied - exactly as in Chinese chess.
  • Elephant moves one step orthogonally then two steps diagonally outwards. Either intermediate point blocks it. It reaches the whole board: there is no river to stop it.
  • Soldier moves one step forward or sideways, never backwards, and captures the same way. There is no promotion in Janggi. Inside the enemy palace it may also use the diagonals, as long as they take it forward.

Bikjang: facing Generals

A General may be moved onto the open file of the enemy General, with nothing in between. This is a draw offer: if the opponent does not break the alignment - moving his General away or interposing a piece - the game is drawn.

Passing

A player who has no legal move does not lose and is not stalemated: he passes, and the game goes on.
