Khan's Chess pits an ordinary chess army, the Kingdom, against an all-cavalry one, the Horde. Couch Tomato designed it in 2023-2024, after neural-network testing showed that his earlier Orda Chess was not as balanced as it had seemed - and because a horde of foot soldiers never really suited the Mongol theme.

Every piece of the Horde moves like a knight. What each of them does instead when it captures is what tells them apart: the lancer takes along the lines like a rook, the horse archer along the diagonals like a bishop, the khatun only on the squares next to her, and the scout on the single square straight ahead of it. Only the kheshig - knight and king combined, and the strongest piece on the board - captures where it moves.

Two rules apply to both armies and change how the endgame feels. Stalemate is a loss rather than a draw, and a king that reaches the far rank without ever stepping into check wins on the spot: the campmate. A bare king is never out of resources here.
