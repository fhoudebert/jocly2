Khan's Chess

Khan's Chess is an asymmetric game: the Kingdom (White) is the ordinary chess army, while the Horde (Black) is entirely mounted. It was designed by Couch Tomato in 2023-2024 as a rebalanced successor to Orda Chess, replacing that game's pawns and yurt with the scout and the khatun.

The Horde's pieces stand where their chess counterparts would: lancers in the corners, kheshigs beside them, archers next, khatun and khan in the middle. Its scouts start on the second rank rather than the first. The Kingdom always moves first.

The Horde

Every Horde unit moves like a knight - the Mongol army was cavalry - but most of them capture differently from the way they move. Pieces like that are called divergent; the chess pawn is the familiar example. So a lancer sitting three squares away from your queen on the same file is attacking it, while a lancer a knight's jump away is attacking nothing at all.

Scout (S)

The scout jumps like a knight, but only to the four forward squares, and it attacks a single square straight ahead. That narrow attack makes it much worse than a pawn at defending - scouts cannot form chains - but it crosses the board in half the time, so it threatens promotion early. A scout reaching the last rank promotes, and can only become a khatun.

Khatun (T)

The khatun moves as a knight and captures as a king. Despite occupying the queen's square she is the weakest piece of the back rank, and is at her best on defence. (A khatun is the khan's wife.)

Horse Archer (A)

The archer moves as a knight and captures along the diagonals like a bishop. Because it is not colourbound - it can reach every square of the board - it is worth more than the bishop it faces. Archers fork and skewer at a distance while being impossible to trap by colour.

Kheshig (H)

The kheshig combines the knight and the king, and is the one Horde piece that captures exactly where it moves. It starts on the knight's square but is the strongest unit in the game, and the Horde generally keeps its kheshigs at home until the endgame: mating the khan while a kheshig still guards him is very hard. The kheshigs were the imperial guard of the Mongol royalty.

Lancer (L)

The lancer moves as a knight and captures along the ranks and files like a rook. It is weaker than a rook, and increasingly so as the board empties, since it cannot travel across the board in one move - but it enters play far earlier than a rook does, which is the Horde's compensation.

Rules

  1. The Kingdom (White) always moves first.
  2. The only piece the two armies have in common is the king. The Horde's king is called the khan; the different name and symbol are purely thematic, and it plays exactly like a king.
  3. The Horde cannot castle. The Kingdom castles as in chess.
  4. Pawns promote to any piece, as in chess. Scouts promote to a khatun only.
  5. Campmate: bringing your own king to the far rank - the eighth for the Kingdom, the first for the Horde - wins the game immediately. As ever, the king may not move into check to do it, so the far rank has to be taken, not merely walked to.
  6. Stalemate is a loss for the player who cannot move, rather than a draw as in chess.
  7. Everything else, repetition included, is as in chess.

The two armies are close in strength: the Horde's pieces are individually awkward, but they develop faster and the kheshigs dominate the endgame.
