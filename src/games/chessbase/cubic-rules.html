Cube Chess — Frontier variant

Chess played on the surface of a 4×4×4 cube: six 4×4 panels, 96 squares in all. This implementation now enforces the rules (checks, checkmate, stalemate and legal-move generation all work), so it can be played against the computer as well as human vs human.

Panels and notation

The six panels are numbered 1 to 6. Panel 1 is the White home base, panel 6 the Black home base; panels 2–5 wrap around the sides. A square is named <panel><row><column>, with rows A–D and columns 1–4 — for example 1C2 is panel 1, row C, column 2 (the White king's starting square).

Frontier walls

Along each home base, the two edges where squares of the same colour meet are closed by a wall: no piece may move across them. These walls fence off the two base panels from the side panels along those edges, while leaving the pawns' corridors and the equator open. A rook or queen sliding along an open ring travels the full great circle (16 squares) until it is blocked or reaches a wall.

Initial setup

Each side has 16 men: 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, 1 queen and 1 king. Pawns occupy the two open edges of the home panel (rows A and D); the pieces fill the two inner rows (B and C).

How the pieces move

  • Rook — slides along the cube's orthogonal great circles, turning naturally across panel edges.
  • Bishop — slides diagonally across the surface, wrapping around edges. It cannot pass through a cube corner (a vertex where three panels meet): a diagonal aimed at a vertex stops there.
  • Queen — rook and bishop combined.
  • Knight — the usual (2,1) leap, transported across edges; it jumps over pieces but not over walls.
  • King — one square in any of its (up to eight) surface directions.
  • Pawn — advances straight along its meridian away from its home panel, one square at a time (two on its first move, from either home row). It captures onto the two forward diagonal squares.

Castling

Short-side castling only. White plays King 1C2→1C4 with Rook 1C4→1C3; Black plays King 6B2→6B4 with Rook 6B4→6B3. As in orthodox chess it is illegal while in, through, or into check, and the squares between king and rook must be empty.

Promotion

A pawn promotes to queen, rook, bishop or knight when it reaches row B or C of the opposing home panel (panel 6 for White, panel 1 for Black).

Not yet enforced

For now this implementation omits three finer points of the full Frontier ruleset: en passant capture, the special sideways pawn move along an opponent's wall on the side panels, and any further castling refinements. These may be added later.

Échecs cubiques — variante Frontier (français)

Jeu d'échecs sur la surface d'un cube 4×4×4 : six panneaux de 4×4, 96 cases. Les règles sont désormais appliquées (échec, mat, pat et coups légaux), donc jouable aussi contre l'ordinateur.

Les panneaux 1 à 6 : le 1 est la base des Blancs, le 6 celle des Noirs. Une case se note <panneau><rangée A–D><colonne 1–4> (ex. 1C2). Le long de chaque base, les deux arêtes où se rejoignent des cases de même couleur sont fermées par un mur infranchissable.

Chaque camp a 8 pions (rangées A et D), 2 tours, 2 cavaliers, 2 fous, 1 dame, 1 roi (rangées B et C). La tour parcourt les grands cercles ; le fou glisse en diagonale mais ne franchit pas un sommet du cube ; le cavalier saute les pièces mais pas les murs ; le pion avance le long de son méridien (deux cases au premier coup) et prend en diagonale. Roque petit côté seulement (Blancs 1C2→1C4, Noirs 6B2→6B4). Promotion sur les rangées B ou C de la base adverse. Non encore gérés : la prise en passant et le coup latéral spécial du pion.
